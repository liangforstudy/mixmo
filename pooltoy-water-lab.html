Water



Moving the slider pours water in or out through his belly button, once. He is sealed and firm, so the same amount of air goes the other way.


The surface sits square to the gravity the water feels (gravity minus his acceleration) on a springy, lightly damped swing paced by each part's size. Spinning and hard landings push it up the walls as a bowl; in the air it is weightless. Waves run across the body's surface, and each limb holds its own water.





Deflate





Presses him flat front to back, like a cookie (step one of deflating; Capsule and GLB model for now; on a model each part goes as flat as its water lets it, and a tail goes flat side to side). Vinyl folds, it doesn't stretch: the skin keeps its length round him, so he gets wider as he gets thinner, up to about 1.57 times. The water holds him open: Flat stops where the room left is just his water, and the level rises as he flattens. The air goes out and comes back as if a valve were open (real valves come later).





Test moves (what to look for)

    

  • Walk or run, then Stop: the water should pile against the front wall, swing back and settle. The pile starts at the wall and a wave runs back across the surface.
    • 

  • Turns and Spin: water leans to the outside of a turn, and rides up the walls in a spin.
    • 

  • Jump and Drop: weightless in the air, then a surge up the walls on landing: rings run in from the walls and meet in the middle.
    • 

  • Flip: upside down, the water pours to the lower end (with separate limbs: out of the legs, through the body, into the head). Where it comes in through a joint, it ripples the surface.
    • 

  • Standing still: it must settle flat and stay there.
    • 



Numbers in the view: tilt of the surface; its height above his base; how far the bowl rises at the wall (20 out from the middle); the tallest wave on the body's surface; his acceleration sideways and upwards in g; the gravity the water feels and how fast it sloshes; how full each part is; the water and the air inside him in litres.





Water: tuning

Waves on the body's surface: a small grid of water columns (the wave equation) that the tilting surface, landings and water coming through the joints all disturb, bouncing off the body's actual walls. Damping like real water: big sloshes break and die quickly, small ones linger, and small or shallow parts settle sooner than the body.




Water in


Each limb its own: the body, head, arms, legs and tail each hold their own water with their own surface. Water only passes between a limb and the body through the joint, when it reaches it, so a tilted surface in the body no longer fills an arm from nowhere. Water that has run into a hanging arm stays there until he is tipped. One shared: one surface cuts through everything (the first version; no waves).
